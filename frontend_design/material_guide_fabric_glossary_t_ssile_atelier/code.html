Section 01 // Botanical Bast

Pure Flax & Linens

Derived from the sclerenchyma fibers of Linum usitatissimum. Linen possesses unparalleled tensile rigidity, breathability, and natural antibacterial properties, developing fluid suppleness across decades of laundering.

Pressing Technique

Always press linen slightly damp with a heavy dry iron (or moderate steam) against a tailor's tailstock or pressing mitt. Clamp immediately with an untreated beechwood tailor's clapper for 7–10 seconds to set the fold line razor-sharp.

Micro-Structure Analysis

Wet-Spun vs. Dry-Spun Flax Fibers

Wet spinning passes flax slivers through a heated water bath at 60°C to soften natural pectins, producing ultra-uniform, luminescent threads (above 40 lea). Dry spinning preserves the raw irregular lignin joints, yielding tactile, textured coarse goods.

Section 02 // Keratin Fibers

Super Woolens & Worsteds

The pinnacle of traditional British and Biellese tailoring cloth. Merino wool fibers feature natural microscopic scale structures (cuticles) that afford natural memory, elasticity, and steam malleability.

Woolen vs. Worsted

Worsted: Fibers are combed parallel before twisting, removing short hairs for smooth, crisp, crease-resistant drape.

Woolen: Carded without parallel combing; traps air for fuzzy, sponge-like thermal tweeds, flannels, and velours.

IWTO Standard Grade

Deciphering the "Super S" System

Higher numbers indicate finer fiber microns, yielding silkier hand feel at the cost of durability and wrinkle recovery.

S 110s 18.25 μ

S 180s+ 14.75 μ
Ceremonial Sovereign

Approaching cashmere micron level. Extreme delicacy; prone to shine under heavy dry heat.

Steam Malleability & Shrink-Pressing

Unlike cellulose fibers, wool fibers contain disulfide cross-links that relax under moist heat (160°C) and lock into place as they cool. This allows master tailors to literally curve straight cloth edges to match the shoulder blade or hollow back without darting.

Tailor's Rule of Thumb Always place a pure cotton or linen pressing cloth between iron soleplate and wool surface to prevent thermal glazing (scorch marks).
straighten

Internal Chest Canvas Compatibility

The weight of your shell fabric dictates the canvas tier. An ultra-dense 400gsm flannel can easily absorb heavy hair-canvas, whereas a featherweight 210gsm Super 150s worsted requires single-ply lightweight goat-hair canvas to avoid show-through on lapel rolls.

Basting Tension Pad-stitch with loose silk twist thread (size 40) at 45-degree angles to maintain kinetic chest expansion.
Section 03 // The Architecture

Natural Interfacings & Canvas

The concealed soul of a tailored jacket. Natural interfacings give structure, contour, and roll without relying on petrochemical synthetic fusible adhesives that bubble and perish over time.

Atelier Commitment

We strictly stock unbleached, glue-free canvas woven on traditional narrow shuttle looms in Veneto and Yorkshire.

Soft unbleached linen warp combined with cotton weft. Extends from the shoulder seam down through the entire jacket forepart to stabilize front edges.

Floating Construction vs. Synthetic Fusible Webbing

Full Floating Canvas (Atelier Standard)

Attached exclusively with tension-free basting stitches. Moves independently from the outer fabric, conforming progressively to the owner’s body contours with heat and wear. Lifespan: decades. Dry-cleanable indefinitely without blistering.

Commercial Fusible Webbing (Mass Market)

Heat-activated polyamide glue melted into the fiber weave under pressure rollers. Restricts cloth drape, creates a stiff unnatural sheen, and inevitably delaminates (bubbles) after repeated exposure to dry-cleaning solvents.

Section 04 & 05 // Specialized Fibers

Mulberry Silks & Raw Cottons

Continuous protein filaments and long-staple seed hairs. Each requires distinct cutting layout protocols, grain alignment, and hand tension.

Mulberry Bombyx Mori

Mulberry Habotai & Silk Crepe

Measured in Momme (mm; 1 mm = 4.340 g/m²). For tailored linings, 16mm to 19mm provides effortless slip without clinging static. Always cut single-layer using serrated dressmaking shears or rotary blades on tissue paper backing.

Gossypium Barbadense

Japanese Selvedge Twill & Drill

Steep 3/1 right-hand twills create rigid diagonal ribs that resist wind and abrasion. Always allow for up to 8% shrinkage along the warp direction if working with untreated loomstate (kibata) selvedge cotton.
